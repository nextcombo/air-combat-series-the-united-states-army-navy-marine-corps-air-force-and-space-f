# [Air Combat Series] The United States Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force jointly release "Multi-Service Air Control Communication Tactics, Technologies, and Procedures"

The new regulations for multi-service air control communication by the US military have been released! On October 9, 2024, the United States Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, and Space Force jointly issued "Multi-Service Air Control Communications Tactics, Technologies, and Procedures," serving as a "universal language manual" in the field of multi-service air combat. The updated content directly affects the synergy efficiency of joint air missions in the US military, holding immense reference value for research in the defense sector.
I. Core Background of the Document: The "Communication Foundation" for Inter-Service Collaboration
The document not only unified the cross-service communication standards but also linked with the intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) system. It can integrate real-time intelligence from RQ-4, RC-135, U-2 reconnaissance aircraft, UAS, space, and cyberspace platforms, providing a unified battlefield situation map for combat personnel, significantly enhancing multi-domain coordination efficiency.
II. Key Updates and Highlights: More in line with modern air combat needs. Compared to the previous version in 2021, this new version has been optimized specifically for its core changes include:
★  
Terminology and Definitions Update: Replacing "package commander" with "team lead" to better align with the flexible combat formation requirements; updating the LOWDOWN situational awareness priority to clarify the corresponding relationship between air-to-air interception phases and communication priorities, preventing coordination confusion.
★  
Added Key Content: Supplement the official definition of Battle Management Areas (BMA) to resolve the vagueness of cross-service combat zone delineation; introduce WAVE COMM communication standardization specifications and examples to unify the multi-wave combat communication process; add two types of flight in-flight reporting (INFLTREP) templates, covering fighter and non-fighter mission scenarios; supplement the new legends for PICTURE tactical air situation maps, optimize the definition, application scenarios, and iconography of SPREAD tactics; clarify the communication process for cruise missile defense (CMD), refine the use rules for exclusive terms such as "COVEY (Missile Cluster)" and "QUAIL (Single Missile)".
★
Redundant Content Reduction: Remove JACKAL related chapters, streamline non-essential processes, focus on core communication efficiency, and adapt to the fast-paced requirements of modern aerial warfare.
III. Core Content Framework: Six major modules cover all scenarios of communication (deepening practical details). The document structure is clear, progressing from basic rules to practical combat processes, with each chapter's core practical points as follows:
1. Overview (Chapter 1): Clarify the "applicable boundaries and core subjects" of communication.
Airspace control dual modes: Clarify the applicable scenarios for "active control" (relying on real-time sensor recognition, tracking, and guidance) and "program control" (relying on preset rules, communication protocols, and airspace control measures), the former used in high-intensity confrontation environments, the latter adapted to low threat or sensor-limited scenarios.
★
TAC C2 functions and military configurations: The core responsibilities of TAC C2 include ensuring troop delivery, providing threat warnings, maintaining situational awareness, and coordinating airspace conflicts; specific military configurations for each service are clearly defined—the Navy has E-2 early warning aircraft, Nimitz/Ford class carriers, the Air Force has AWACS early warning aircraft, Control Report Centers (CRCs), and the Marine Corps has Tactical Air Operations Centers (TAOCs), while the Army does not have a dedicated TAC C2 entity.
★
Core Assumption: It is clear that all parties involved in the operation must adhere to the rules of engagement (ROE) and airspace control plans (ACP) issued by the Joint Force Commander (JFC), with authority delegated by the Area Air Defense Commander (AADC). 
2. Tactical Management Communication (Chapter 2): Solidifying the "Processes and Standards" of Basic Communication ★  
Communication Priorities: Adhere strictly to the sequence of "Flight Safety and Emergency > Kill Confirmation > Defense/Threat Alarm > Situation Awareness Request > Tactical Air Traffic Report > Tactical Management Update", ensuring critical information is prioritized for transmission.  
★  
Four Major Transmission Types: Instructive transmission (clearly specifying the call sign of the recipient), inquiry transmission (format as "Called Party + Inquiry Party + Request Content"), notification transmission (only stating the sender), relay transmission (omitting the call sign of the relay party), each type accompanied by standard phrase examples.  
★  
Check-in Process: Adopt the MNOPOPCA mnemonic (Task Number, Number of Planes and Type, Position and Altitude, Ammunition Status, Duty Time, Capability Configuration, Abort Code), divided into "Scheduled Check-in" (AS FRAGGED) and "Exceptional Check-in" (need to specify the number of planes, ammunition/fuel status, capability changes, etc.), UAVs must check-in through tactical chat tools in the same format.  
★  
Key Support Process: The Link 16 data chain must complete three authenticity checks beforehand, failing which a report will be made as "TIMBER SOUR"; Words instructions (by task day number, such as AA01) should include core information such as formation change, frequency switching, threat update, etc., preferably transmitted via digital links.  
3. Coordination of Formations and Direct Air Support (Chapter 3): Ensuring "Critical Nodes" of Collaboration ★  
Formation Pointing: Can be initiated by TAC C2 notifications or Task Commander (MC), including scenarios such as after LOWDOWN reports, during task sequence changes, or when equipment failure occurs, to ensure the integrity of the formation.  
★
LOWDOWN Situational Report: 5 minutes before combat, transmission must include the activation status and location of remote (LRAD), medium range (MRAD), and close range (SHORAD) air defense systems (preferred in BULLSEYE format for Army/Marine rotary aircraft and drones, in MGRS grid for other types). Rank by "closest to friendly forces, closest to attack axis, with the strongest effect".
★
Timeline adjustment: ROLEX is used to adjust the entire formation's mission time (5-minute increment, not cumulative), while SLIP is used to adjust the mission time for individual/group aircraft (not cumulative). Training scenarios need to explain how the vulnerability window changes.
★
Communication band switching (CHATTERMARK): Preset backup bands (different bands preferred), "restored frequency" (such as TAC C2 check-in frequency band), repeat "CHATTERMARK" three times in the original frequency band before switching to the new frequency band, and after confirming that all personnel are present, announce "NET SWEET".
★
Fuel/Ammunition Status: YELLOW indicates that it is about to be insufficient to complete the mission, RED indicates that it cannot complete the mission, and must proactively report to TAC C2, who will coordinate with the replacement force.
4. Aircraft Combat Communications Fundamentals (Chapter 4): Building a "Description Language System" for airborne situations
Core definition: GROUP refers to an air target cluster within 3 nautical miles (default single target, multiple targets require explicit number); PICTURE refers to tactical air situation maps (excluding friendly forces and neutral targets, requiring separate notification).
★
Core information format: When reporting targets, include "Cluster Number + Location (BULLSEYE/BRAA/GEOREF) + Altitude (rounded to thousand feet) + Course/Orientation + Identification Status + Additional Information", where BULLSEYE is the preferred format (bearing angle + distance, e.g., 225/30 means 225 degrees bearing and 30 nautical miles). BRAA is used for exclusive target notifications for individual aircraft.
Five types of notification methods: Core information (for pre-injury engagement), traditional tags (SINGLE GROUP/RANGE/AZIMUTH/VIC, etc., must meet the recognizability of target formations), leading edge notifications (LEADING EDGE, suitable for complex formations), wave notifications (WAVES, used for multiple waves of threats), and formation notifications (PACKAGE, based on geographical boundaries).
★
Supplementary information priority: Collective size (HEAVY indicates more than three aircraft) > Aircraft type (such as FLNKER fighter jets) > Altitude identification (HIGH refers to over 40,000 feet) > Speed identification (FAST is 1.1-1.5 Mach, VERY FAST is above 1.5 Mach) > Target ownership/threat association > Missile hit time.
5. Air-to-air interception communication (Chapter Five): Standardize intercept "full process phrases"
Intercept phase communication priority: Pre-injury engagement stage (TAC C2 primarily, fighter jets secondary), after entering combat (TAC C2 primarily, fighter jets secondary), target aiming/weapon use (mainly fighter jets, TAC C2 secondary), close-range combat (mainly engaged fighters), after combat (detached fighters).
★
Key commands: COMMIT (commit to engage, requires preset conditions such as range, position, threat type, etc., authority can be delegated to MC, team leader or TAC C2), TARGETED (lock onto a target, with data link only need call sign + target tag, without data link need supplementary position), SHOOT (temporary command, for striking non-primary targets, not responsible for long-term aiming), DECLARE (request recognition of a target, needs to specify the target's location or tag, response should include complete core information).
★
Special Scene Handling: Encountering Electromagnetic Attack (EA) requires reporting "STROBE" (Radar Alarm), "MUSIC" (Deceitful Interference), or "METALLICA" (Unable to Aim); Target Disappearance requires distinguishing between "FADED" (30 seconds of no updates) and "VANISHED" (confirmed hit or left blind zone); Near-field sudden threats require using "SNAPLOCK" for notification, with TAC C2 preferring the BRAA format in response.
6. Ground-to-Air Communication (Chapter VI): Connecting the "Task Link" Between Ground and Air
Task Serialization: Coded as "Task Day + two digits", ending with "EOM" for digital commands, including core information such as target type, location, error level, ammunition count, and restrictions.
★
Task Notification Format: Available in full digital (J28.2/J12.0 link), full voice, and semi-digital-voice, with full voice adopting the DLER format (Description, Location, Altitude, Notes/Restrictions), ensuring no information is missed.
★
Ground Threat Notification: Including COLOR/TAG (Confirmation of radar frequency and location), SITREP (Transmitted 5 minutes before combat, containing air defense system status and warning radar status), CAB (Real-time broadcast of active threats in tactical bands), ITW (Warning within 5 nautical miles of threat), and TRESPASS (Command to turn 180 degrees if entering an anti-aircraft area and to report the threat's location).
★
SEAD Suppression Communication: After receiving "DEFENDING" (Defense request), respond with "HARM INBOUND" (Anti-radiation missile launched), "MAGNUM" (Anti-radiation missile about to be launched), "EMPTY" (No relevant radiation sources), "ARIZONA" (No anti-radiation missile), or "CANYON" (Electronic interference), paired with SNIPER (Known range threats) and SLAPSHOT (Unknown range threats) in both instruction formats.
F2T2EA full process: Find (search task allocation), Fix (positioning accuracy report, divided into low/high/capable targeting), Track (tracking responsibility allocation, responding to "TALLY", "CAPTURED" etc.), Target (attack plan formulation, clear sorting, weapon combination, time coordination), Engage (invoking attack with "PUSHING" and attack time), Assess (assessing effect, "SUCCESSFUL" indicates achieving the goal, "FUMBLE" indicates not achieved, can request re-attack).
IV. Document Value: The "Practical Textbook" for U.S. Air Force Coordinated Combat
1  
Comprehensive Coverage: From basic sign-in to formation coordination, to air-to-air interception and ground-to-air strike, each combat phase is equipped with standardized scripts and operational procedures that can be directly implemented.
2  
Adapting to new technologies: Integration with Link 16 data chain, drone tactical chat, and space/network intelligence access, aligning with the modern air combat technology environment.
3  
Scene-specific differentiation: Develop differentiated rules for training/real combat, data link presence or absence, and different military platforms (fighter jets/rotary wing aircraft/unmanned aerial vehicles), highly practical.
For defense researchers, the document not only showcases the synergistic logic of joint air combat operations in the U.S. military but also provides practical details of tactical communication, serving as an important reference for analyzing the U.S. military's air combat capabilities and drawing on collaborative communication experiences.
